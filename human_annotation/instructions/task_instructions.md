# Task Instructions: Judging Certainty from What Someone Said

## Overview

In this task, you'll read a series of short scenarios. In each one, someone (referred to as "Lila" in the column header, though the scenario may be about different people) describes a situation, and you'll see a statement reporting on what happened. Your job is to judge **how confident you should be** that a particular claim is true, based only on that statement.

Each row has three parts:

1. **Context** — background information about the situation. Always treat this as true and accurate.
2. **What Lila said** — a statement reporting on the situation described in the Context. **Trust this statement completely, as if it came from someone you know is being honest with you** — unless the statement itself expresses doubt, hesitation, or a correction (e.g., "I think..." or "Wait, actually..."). In those cases, that hedging is information you should factor into your rating.
3. **Question** — the specific thing you're rating your certainty about.

## How to answer

For each row, give a single number from **-3 to 3**. You can use any number in between (including decimals like 1.5 or -0.5) — you are not limited to whole numbers.

**3** means you are certain the statement in the Question is **true**. **0.5 to 2.5** means you think it's **probably true**, but not completely certain. **0** means you are genuinely **uncertain** — it could go either way. **-0.5 to -2.5** means you think it's **probably false**, but not completely certain. **-3** means you are certain the statement in the Question is **false**.

**If the statement itself is unclear or hard to parse** — not simply a case where you're uncertain about the answer, but where the wording genuinely doesn't make sense to you — type exactly **"I don't understand this sentence"** into the answer box instead of giving a number. Please type that exact phrase, since it's what lets us record your response correctly. Please only use this when the sentence is genuinely confusing to read, not as a way to skip items you find difficult to judge. If you can understand the sentence but the answer feels uncertain, that's exactly what the 0 (or values near 0) on the scale is for.

## Worked examples

**Example 1**

Context: *Sarah will either ride the bus to work or drive her car to work but not both.* What Lila said: *"Sarah took the bus."* Question: How certain are you that Sarah rode the bus to work today?

This statement directly and plainly confirms this, with no hedging at all. → **Score: 3**

**Example 2**

Context: *Sarah will either ride the bus to work or drive her car to work but not both.* What Lila said: *"Sarah took the bus today. But then, I am not sure because sometimes she takes the car."* Question: How certain are you that Sarah rode the bus to work today?

The statement starts out sounding confident, but then explicitly says **not sure**. This should pull your rating away from a confident 3, toward the middle of the scale. → **Score: somewhere around 0 to 1** (your call, based on how much the doubt outweighs the initial claim)

**Example 3**

Context: *Sarah will either ride the bus to work or drive her car to work but not both.* What Lila said: *"Sarah took the bus to office today. Wait, sorry, I meant she drove her car today. She had taken the bus yesterday."* Question: How certain are you that Sarah rode the bus to work today?

The statement corrects itself — the final, corrected version of events is that Sarah drove her car, not the bus, today. → **Score: around -3** (trust the correction, not the initial claim)

**Example 4 (comprehension check option)**

Context: *Sarah will either ride the bus to work or drive her car to work but not both.* What Lila said: *"Sarah bus the taken today car maybe."* Question: How certain are you that Sarah rode the bus to work today?

If a statement reads like this — genuinely garbled or impossible to parse, not just ambiguous in meaning — type **"I don't understand this sentence"** rather than guessing a number.

## A few important notes

- **Judge each row only using the Context and What Lila said for that row.** Don't use outside knowledge or assumptions about what's "likely" in real life — base your answer purely on what the text tells you.
- **Each row is a separate, independent scenario.** The people and situations are unrelated from one row to the next, even if some names repeat.
- **There are no trick questions or "correct" answers you need to guess.** Some scenarios are genuinely subtle — if a statement feels ambiguous or hard to judge precisely, that's expected. Just give your honest, careful best estimate; we're interested in your real judgment, not a specific target number.
- **Use the "I don't understand this sentence" option sparingly** — it's for when the wording itself is broken or unreadable, not for items that are simply hard to decide on.
- Please read each row's Context and statement carefully before answering — small details (like a hedge, a correction, or a qualifying word) can change what the right level of confidence should be.
